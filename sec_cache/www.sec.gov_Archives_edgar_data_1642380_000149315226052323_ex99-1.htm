IMDx Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results and Provides Corporate Update

 

●Progress with FDA review of GraftAssureDx and its planned response to FDA’s request for additional information

 

●Initiated enrollment into GALACTIC registry study from UCLA Medical Center

 

●Revenue from GraftAssureCore lab-developed test expected to build as GALACTIC enrollment grows

 

●Q3 2026 revenue of $559,000; operating expenses decline as FDA-submission costs wind down

 

●iMDx appoints veteran transplant sales leader to prepare for GraftAssureDx commercial launch

 

NASHVILLE, Tenn., Nov. 16, 2026 — Insight Molecular Diagnostics Inc., iMDx, (Nasdaq: IMDX), today published the following letter to shareholders in conjunction with its third quarter results:

 

Fellow shareholders,

 

Innovation and disruption in diagnostics tend to follow a well-known path: A new test starts in a specialized laboratory, becomes used more widely, and eventually becomes a broadly available kit that can be run at local laboratories.

 

There are countless examples of familiar tests that have progressed toward simpler, more accessible test kits. In this classic story of decentralization, we believe that our proprietary GraftAssureDx kits are positioned to lead this inevitable transition in transplanted organ health monitoring, bringing donor-derived cell-free DNA (dd-cfDNA) testing to transplant centers’ own laboratories.

 

In March 2026, GraftAssureDx became, to our knowledge, the first test of its kind submitted to the FDA – and we provide an update on the status of that review below.

 

We believe the organ transplant industry is prepared to embrace decentralized molecular testing for organ rejection, as it already has for organ matching, also known as HLA typing.

 

In recent quarters, our confidence in our thesis has grown, based on the direct and enthusiastic feedback of transplant surgeons and lab directors at leading hospitals in the U.S., Europe, and Asia. It also has been fueled by our deepening conversations with large reference laboratories. These well-known labs have wide national footprints and seek to deliver to their customers an easier way to access transplanted organ health testing.

 

 
 

 

Our investment thesis has also been strengthened by the published clinical evidence supporting the clinical utility of our chosen biomarker, dd-cfDNA, as well as our belief in our product fit within the transplant industry. We believe we entered the fourth quarter with a more favorable reimbursement environment (due to MolDX’s revision of its local coverage determination in support of surveillance testing), a growing body of independent clinical evidence (as presented by three independent institutions during the 2026 American Society for Histocompatibility and Immunogenetics annual meeting), and a commercial organization preparing to drive growth.

 

Finally, our GALACTIC registry study is now enrolling patients, and we congratulate UCLA Medical Center for enrolling our first several patients. We expect sample collection and the related GraftAssureCore testing revenue to build over the coming months as enrollment grows and additional sites are activated.

 

FDA review and timeline update

 

Since our August update, we have continued to advance our dialogue with the FDA.

 

Our FDA reviewers have been engaged, and we continue to be grateful for their responsiveness. The complexity and diligence of the de novo regulatory approval process speaks to the nature of the objective we have set for ourselves: We seek to be the first company to gain U.S. marketing authorization of our dd-cfDNA kidney transplant rejection test.

 

We estimate that reference laboratories perform hundreds of thousands of dd-cfDNA tests each year in the U.S., with Medicare reimbursement available through its MolDX program. These lab-developed tests are performed in CLIA-certified laboratories but, to our knowledge, kidney allograft tests have never cleared FDA marketing authorization.

 

Based on our recent dialogue with the FDA combined with the head-to-head studies being run in the field, we believe that our team and our underlying GraftAssure technology can generate data that meets the FDA’s requirements. If authorized, we believe GraftAssureDx could help improve transplant patient care by formalizing dd-cfDNA testing standards through FDA’s rigorous review. Authorization would also enable use of this technology by local laboratories.

 

In late July, the FDA completed the current phase of its substantive review of GraftAssureDx and requested additional information, which is common for a de novo submission. The information request relates primarily to the design and analysis of our clinical validation study, with FDA’s preliminary view being that the submitted clinical data do not yet support our proposed intended use.

 

In late September, we met with the FDA to discuss our approach to addressing the additional information request. The agency has provided both verbal and written feedback to help guide our response.

 

 
 

 

We see two external processes that factor into the timing for our response. The first is an independent study to further demonstrate the accuracy and consistency of our test results. The second is a review of biopsy slides, for which we have secured two well-known expert pathologists, to establish the diagnoses against which our test results will be evaluated. We currently expect both processes to be completed before the end of the year and expect to submit our complete response before the January 21, 2027 deadline.

 

The FDA has a targeted review timeline of 150 days for Class II de novo submissions. Since we received the additional information request in late July, the review clock has been paused. We estimate that approximately half of the review days remain and expect the clock to restart upon submission of our complete response.

 

Real-world assay performance on research-use-only mode

 

In field testing, our assay continues to perform well in real-world settings. A large transplant center recently presented data at the American Society for Histocompatibility and Immunogenetics (ASHI) meeting comparing GraftAssure’s analytical accuracy and clinical performance with currently available competitive send-out assays.

 

In one example, a single sample run on a STAT basis had a turn-around time of approximately 6 ½ hours!

 

STAT (from Latin statim = immediately) means that the doctor wants the test to be performed at once, and using GraftAssure, the large center lab was able to do so.

 

Compared to the lengthiness of sending out a test and waiting up to a week for a result, the difference with in-house testing has the potential to be clinically compelling and impactful. This field testing builds on head-to-head data presented earlier this year by Tampa General Hospital, which we view is favorable to our assay and can be viewed here on YouTube.

 

Perhaps the most exciting news coming out of the ASHI meeting is how close transplant centers are to launching in-house dd-cfDNA testing services in the U.S. Several U.S. hospital laboratories have completed analytical and clinical validation and are moving forward towards pursuing reimbursement.

 

Several of these centers have indicated to us that they are committed to in-house testing and are targeting to launch in 2027. We believe that demand for kitted tests will only grow after the successful launch of services at the initial transplant centers.

 

We are also thrilled that ASHI itself, a leading medical society in the field, is so supportive of in-house testing. ASHI is identifying proficiency testing suppliers for in-house dd-cfDNA testing, laying the groundwork for broad adoption of our test kits at HLA labs across the US.

 

Other highlights since our August 2026 update:

On October 14, 2026, we announced the appointment of Donnie Purto as Senior Vice President of Sales to lead our commercialization efforts. Donnie will be focusing on go-to-market preparation while GraftAssureDx is under FDA medical device review and is awaiting regulatory authorization.

 

 
 

 

Mr. Purto is an accomplished sales leader with more than 20 years of experience building and leading high-performing sales organizations across diagnostics, life sciences and healthcare. During a decade at Natera, Inc., he succeeded in leadership roles spanning sales, training, oncology, transplant, and women’s health. Since our August update, under Donnie’s leadership we have made significant progress in building the commercial foundation for our GraftAssure family of tests. We have optimized our sales operations and are enhancing our customer experience by pursuing connectivity solutions for send-out testing to our Franklin, Tennessee-based clinical (CLIA) lab, for GraftAssureCore. We are also expanding reimbursement support beyond MolDX-covered jurisdictions through additional resources and payer engagement efforts. As a reminder, GraftAssureCore is the lab-developed test version of our technology that is run in our CLIA-certified lab. After laboratories gain experience using GraftAssureCore, we believe they may be likely to adopt GraftAssureDx more rapidly once it receives FDA marketing authorization.

 

Disciplined cost structure

 

GraftAssureDx’s disciplined commercial strategy is supported by the U.S. kidney transplant market’s concentration. The top 100 transplant centers perform about 80% of U.S. kidney transplants and the top 50 centers perform about 53%. This concentration allows us to efficiently target key accounts and decision makers with a focused, highly specialized commercial team.

 

As a result, we believe that we can maintain a disciplined cost structure while we pursue achieving meaningful market penetration. Further, knowing we can start with an efficient base, we plan to scale our commercial organization prudently and only expand headcount as revenue growth and market adoption justify additional investment, ensuring that commercial spending remains aligned with business performance and shareholder value creation.

The event featured Max Jacob Liebo, M.D. (Loyola University Medical Center) in a discussion with iMDx CEO Josh Riggs, and was moderated by iMDx VP of Medical Affairs, Nick Ioannou, M.D., MHA.

High-level contextual overview for investors:

 

iMDx aims to deliver proven, more affordable, faster tests that can be run in-house at local transplant center laboratories. We have designed the GraftAssureDx molecular test to be sold as a test kit so that transplant center laboratories can run tests locally. We believe that by running tests locally, laboratories can deliver critical test results to the physicians of transplant patients more quickly than can be done with the currently available send-out tests. We are now seeking FDA marketing authorization to sell these kits in the U.S. If GraftAssure technology becomes available commercially as a test kit, we believe it could significantly improve how transplanted organ rejection is monitored.

 

Over time, iMDx sees three potential paradigm shifts in transplanted organ health monitoring:

 

●Bringing testing closer to the patient: The first is a shift in the location of where donor-derived cell-free DNA (dd-cfDNA) testing is performed – migrating out of a send-out service model and into hospital-based laboratories that can deliver results locally. iMDx seeks to demonstrate that in-house testing is better for patients and physicians. (As a reminder, dd-cfDNA is an established biomarker for assessing the health of a transplanted organ through a simple blood draw.)

 

●Expanding the clinical role of dd-cfDNA: The second shift is the growing potential for dd-cfDNA testing, powered by digital PCR technology, to support earlier detection of allograft injury, longitudinal monitoring of transplant health, and assessment of response to emerging anti-rejection therapies.

 

●Advancing from rule-out testing to comprehensive decision support: The third shift is the expansion of dd-cfDNA testing from mainly being used to rule out patients’ need for confirmatory biopsy testing, to also being used proactively to predict whether a patient may be progressing toward organ rejection. This important shift is enabled by GraftAssure’s ability to measure both dd-cfDNA percentage and absolute, true concentrations as copies per milliliter of plasma.

Third Quarter 2026 financial overview

 

●In Q3 2026, our revenues were $559,000, compared to approximately $260,000 in Q3 2025. Our strategic goal is to sell diagnostic test kits for clinical use, and as we await potential FDA authorization, we remain essentially “pre-revenue.”

 

○Most of our revenues were derived from laboratory services performed at our clinical (CLIA) laboratory in Tennessee. Our laboratory services are performed at the request of select clients, and we see our laboratory services revenue as a testament to our team’s ability to achieve the on-time delivery of clear, scientifically sound, and accurate data sets to our clients.

 

○We sold $21,000 in research-use-only kits in the quarter, compared to zero in Q3 2025. As previously communicated, we do not expect material revenue on our kitted product sales until after we have achieved regulatory authorization to market GraftAssureDx.

 

●We reported gross profit of $420,000 in Q3 2026, representing a 75.1% gross margin, compared to gross profit of $139,000 and a 53.5% gross margin in Q3 2025.

 

●In Q3 2026, operating expenses were $4.1 million compared to $11.2 million in Q3 2025. Third quarter operating expenses included a non-cash gain of $4.1 million from a change in the fair value of our contingent consideration, as well as $820,000 in non-cash stock-based compensation expenses and $623,000 in non-cash depreciation and amortization expenses.

 

○Excluding the impact of these non-cash net adjustments in Q3 2026, our non-GAAP operating expenses1 decreased 15% sequentially over the second quarter of 2026, and remained flat year over year at $6.7 million compared with Q3 2025.

 

○The sequential decline in Q3 2026 non-GAAP operating expenses1 reflected the winding down of costs associated with our GraftAssureDx submission.

 

○In Q3 2026, research and development expenses decreased 15% sequentially, or by about $693,000, to $3.8 million, compared to $4.5 million in Q2 2026 and $3.9 million in Q3 2025.

 

■The sequential decline in research and development expenses, as expected, reflects a reduction in consulting costs, software development costs, and clinical trial and registry service fees, which are affiliated with our GraftAssureDx submission to the FDA.

iMDx Transplant Products and Product Candidates in Development

 

iMDx’s flagship transplant testing technology quantifies a molecular biomarker known as donor-derived cell-free DNA (dd-cfDNA). The Company’s scientists in Germany and the U.S. have played a critical role over the past decade in developing the science that helped establish dd-cfDNA as a trusted biomarker of transplant rejection. iMDx is commercializing this technology using a market-disruptive business strategy. Under the GraftAssure™ brand, iMDx’s transplant diagnostics include the following:

 

●GraftAssureCore – The company’s laboratory-developed test (LDT), currently reimbursed by CMS MolDX at $2,753 per result and performed at iMDx’s CLIA-certified laboratory in Franklin, Tennessee.

 

●GraftAssureIQ – A research-use-only (RUO) kit intended and labeled for non-clinical applications.

 

●GraftAssureDx – The in vitro diagnostic (IVD) kit currently under FDA review for use in clinical decision-making.

INSIGHT MOLECULAR DIAGNOSTICS INC.

RECONCILIATION OF NON-GAAP FINANCIAL MEASURES

NON-GAAP NET LOSS, ADJUSTED EBITDA LOSS AND NON-GAAP OPERATING EXPENSES

 

In addition to financial results determined in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (“GAAP”), this press release also includes non-GAAP financial measures (as defined under SEC Regulation G), including non-GAAP operating expenses, non-GAAP net loss, non-GAAP net loss per share, Adjusted EBITDA Loss, and free cash flow. Non-GAAP operating expenses and non-GAAP net loss exclude the change in fair value of contingent consideration, stock-based compensation, and depreciation and amortization. Adjusted EBITDA Loss is defined as net loss before interest, income taxes, depreciation, amortization, stock-based compensation, change fair value of contingent consideration, and other non-operating items. Free cash flow is defined as net cash used in operating activities less capital expenditures. Management uses these measures to evaluate operating performance and cash requirements and believes they are useful to investors in assessing our ongoing performance by excluding certain non-cash and other non-operating items that may obscure the underlying trends in the business.

 

We believe that disclosing the adjusted amounts is helpful in assessing our ongoing performance, providing insight into the Company’s core operating performance by excluding certain non-cash and other non-operating items that may obscure the underlying trends in the business. These non-GAAP financial measures, when viewed in a reconciliation to respective GAAP financial measures, provide an additional way of viewing the Company’s results of operations and factors and trends affecting the Company’s business. These non-GAAP financial measures should be considered as a supplement to, and not as a substitute for, or superior to, the respective financial results presented in accordance with GAAP.
